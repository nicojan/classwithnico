A Student’s Message

Why motivation matters more than perfection in language learning

  • teaching
  • English learning
  • motivation
  • student-success

When I thought about starting this blog, I was unsure how to begin. I wanted the first post to reflect what matters most in my teaching, not just an announcement or a list of services. Then last week, I received a message from one of my grade 7 students.

He had just moved to the United States and was still adjusting to a new school and city. His English is around an A2 level so spelling and grammar often pose challenges, yet he chose to communicate me in English anyway outside of our classes.

In that short note, he told me he enjoys our classes. It was not polished or perfect but it carried weight. What mattered was not the form but the effort, and the simple wish to connect.

Why Motivation Matters

Mistakes are part of language learning but they are not the whole story. What truly shapes progress is the motivation that drives a student to keep trying, even when the path feels uncertain.

A 2024 study in BMC Psychology explored what motivates English learners. The researchers found that many students are guided less by external rewards, such as grades or approval, and more by internal satisfaction and interest. They also noted that background factors like age or field of study made little difference in levels of motivation (Kashefian Naeeini, Shokrpour, & Pakdel, 2024). In other words, every learner has the potential to develop strong internal motivation when the conditions are right.

What It Looks Like in Practice

My student’s message is one example of how this motivation appears in daily life. He was not required to write, nor was he completing an assignment. He reached out because he wanted to, taking a risk to express himself in a language he is still learning. That willingness to try, despite mistakes, is what makes long-term growth possible.

For students who are new to a country, these moments are often the turning points. They are navigating an unfamiliar language and culture at the same time. What sustains them is not flawless grammar but the sense that their efforts are noticed and valued.

How Parents Can Help

Parents play a vital role in building this confidence. A few consistent habits can make English less intimidating and more a part of daily life.

  • Praise the effort, not only the outcome.
  • Invite English into small routines, such as ordering food or writing simple notes.
  • Treat mistakes as a natural step, not as a failure.
  • Ask your child about what they are learning, and listen closely to what they share.

These practices create an environment where trying feels safe and progress becomes visible.

How Class with Nico Supports Students

In my lessons, I focus on creating that same environment of trust and encouragement. Students are given space to speak first, without fear of being corrected too soon. Then I guide them in refining their language, showing progress clearly from week to week so that they have ocular proof of their growth.

My aim is for each student to believe their voice matters. A child who feels heard will take more risks, and with every risk comes the chance to learn.

Closing Thoughts

Learning a new language is demanding work. It takes patience, courage, and the willingness to face uncertainty. Yet real success begins not with a perfect sentence but with the decision to speak, to write, to reach out.

If you would like your child to grow more confident in English, to take risks and see steady progress, I would be glad to speak with you. At Class with Nico, every student is encouraged to use their voice and to see it as something worth developing.
